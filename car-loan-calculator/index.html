How this car loan calculator works

A car loan is usually repaid in equal monthly payments over a fixed number of years. You enter the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the term. The calculator then shows your monthly payment, the total interest, the total you will pay and a full payment schedule. You can also add an extra monthly payment to see how much sooner you could finish.

The loan amount is what you actually borrow. That is the price of the car, minus your down payment and any trade-in value, plus any fees you choose to finance. Borrowing less means paying less interest, so a bigger down payment helps.

A worked example

Take a car loan of $25,000 at 7% a year for 5 years. These are the starting values in the $ USD calculator above.

  • The monthly payment is about $495.03.
  • Over 60 payments you pay about $29,702 in total. About $4,702 of that is interest.
  • In the first month, the interest is $25,000 × 7% ÷ 12, which is $145.83. So about $349.20 of your first payment reduces the loan.
  • Now change the term to 4 years. The payment rises to about $598.65, but the total interest falls to about $3,735.

The shorter loan costs about $104 more each month, and it saves about $967 in interest.

Tips for a cheaper car loan

  • Make a larger down payment. It lowers the amount you borrow and the interest you pay.
  • Choose the shortest term you can afford. A long term makes the payment look small, but the total cost is higher.
  • Compare offers. Put two loans side by side in the loan comparison calculator. Compare total interest, not just the monthly payment.
  • Pay extra when you can. The loan prepayment calculator shows how much interest an extra payment saves.
  • Budget for the whole car. Insurance, fuel, maintenance and taxes are not part of the loan, so add them to your monthly budget.
  • Ask about fees. Processing fees and early repayment charges change the real cost. This calculator does not include them.

Frequently asked questions

How is a car loan payment calculated?

The payment comes from the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the number of months. The calculator finds the equal monthly payment that repays the loan with interest by the last month.

Should I enter the car price or the loan amount?

Enter the loan amount, which is the car price minus your down payment and any trade-in value. Add any fees that you are financing, because they are borrowed money too.

Is a longer car loan better?

A longer loan lowers the monthly payment, but you pay interest for more months, so the total cost is higher. A shorter loan has a higher payment and costs less in total.

What else does a car cost besides the loan?

Insurance, fuel, maintenance, taxes, registration and parking all add to the cost. This calculator only shows the loan itself, so add these costs to your budget separately.

Can I pay off my car loan early?

Often yes, and an extra monthly payment shortens the loan and saves interest. Some lenders charge a fee for early repayment, so check your loan agreement first.
